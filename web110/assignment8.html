  As our lives become more intertwined with the online world, so too does much of our sensitive information. Because a bad password can become the equivalent of an insecure lock on your door, it is important to learn about the different digital security threats and protections that exist; with this week’s activities, progress towards that can be made. This is especially true for activities “Complete the following tutorials” (activity 1), “Research password managers and multi-factor authentication” (activity 4), and “Test your firewall...” (activity 5). Serving as a general overview of internet security, the first activity laid down the foundations of keeping information safe online. WIth tips on creating passwords, refining privacy settings, and identifying malicious content, it provided useful guidance for future internet usage. Whether review or first time learning, such information is crucial for protecting information, and I am grateful for the overview.

  Unlike the previous activity which focused more on the general picture, activity 4 delved more into the details of one specific security feature -- passwords. There seems to be a password required for everything these days, and for good reason. However, with each new account, such information becomes harder and harder to remember, often leading to increasingly lax attention to making keys strong. Password managers (a tool mentioned in the activity) can potentially solve both problems. Doing the remembering for the user, the program makes forgetting passwords less of an issue. Consequently, users may be less discouraged to use a new password for each account. In addition, some managers provide generators, allowing for strong password creation. Even still, passwords aren’t infallible, making two-factor authentication much needed. As I continue to use the internet, I will inevitably need to create more passwords. With the tools discussed in the activity, I can be more assured that the keys I use are not only strong (and remembered), but fortified by a back-up security system.

  Even when passwords are strong, there are various other vulnerabilities to security that can threaten computers and their data. Checking that other protections, such as firewalls, are in a proper working state is vital. Knowing the weaknesses in your safeguards is an important step towards fortifying them, making activity 5 an important one to keep in mind and on hand.
