  During this set of activities, I was able to both review and learn numerous important details regarding web browsers. While I believe they all served to help strengthen my foundations, the ones that stood out to me were “Configure browser settings - Brave,” “Practice using web browsers - Opera,” and “Configure browser extensions and add-ons.” Despite using Chrome for the majority of my life, I discovered Brave a few years ago and have been loyal to it since. Because it is such an integral part of my online experience, knowing the ins and outs of how to personalize it and make it as efficient as possible is very important. I somehow made it multiple years without becoming aware of the Speedreader feature -- one I will definitely be using from here on out -- which streamlines the contents of pages. Similarly, the Way Back Machine integration flew under my radar before, but I am very glad to be aware of it now. These (in addition to multiple other features I learned about) will hopefully make my future browsing faster, easier, and more efficient.

  As mentioned above, I tend to stick with a single browser. Unfortunately, the neglect of other browsers diminishes my knowledge of them. Because of this, being able to dive into the intricacies of unknown platforms, specifically Opera, was insightful. I was able to learn about various features, such as visual bookmarks, accommodating sidebars, and general browser customizations. Through learning about the differences and similarities between it and Brave, I now feel more informed about my choice. Additionally, if I were to ever consider a different browser in the future, I am more aware of my options. Even if I were to remain loyal to Brave, however, knowing how to use different browsers will still come in handy. Being flexible is a valuable skill to have, and if ever presented with a situation in which I am unable to have my preference of browser, I will be more comfortable because of this activity.

  Extensions are valuable tools when it comes to web browsing, but I have not really had much experience in utilizing them. For that reason, I enjoyed learning how to best make use of them and what options were available to me. In researching the most popular extensions for Brave, the top two (on Browserfly) for productivity were revealed to be Grammarly and Todoist, helpful tools that would be valuable to have at the tip of my fingers. I have used extensions before, but I do not know why I did so sparingly; this activity has made me consider changing that while also providing me with knowledge on how best to do so.
